# Potential value and evaluation

This pilot may make mathematical-claim audits more precise by preserving intermediate vetoes, scope restrictions, non-promoting tests and documentary corrections. Its unit of observation is a sourced claim-state transition, with separate epistemic, documentary, diffusion and workflow states.

## Potential uses

1. Locate when and why a claim changed scope, rather than reconstructing the history from a final paper alone.
2. Distinguish mathematical refutation from a missing witness, a publication decision or a provenance correction.
3. Assign bounded verification tasks and preserve disagreements for independent review.
4. Provide training examples about evidence, conditional statements and AI-assisted research accountability.

These are proposed uses, not measured benefits or evidence of external adoption. Generic provenance, claim/evidence models and audit trails predate this work. Any originality remains limited to this particular protocol/corpus combination and subject to prior-art review.

## Tests that would support practical value

| Question | Evaluation | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Can independent readers reconstruct the coding? | Independently code at least 6 of 21 events; keep disagreements and report agreement under a predeclared rule | Agreement alone does not establish mathematical truth |
| Does the protocol transfer? | Apply the declared codebook to an external corpus and record needed changes | This pilot is not representative |
| Does it help auditing? | Compare predeclared audit tasks with and without the protocol; measure correct findings, witness retrieval and attribution errors | No causal effect is measured by the current retrospective corpus |
| Can critiques change the record? | Publish sourced corrections as later events or version notes | Preserve the frozen historical snapshot |

The available evidence is the executable descriptive pilot and its structural checks. Those checks validate data invariants; they do not certify every underlying claim, model reliability, a new provenance ontology or progress toward an open mathematical problem.
