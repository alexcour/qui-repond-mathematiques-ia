# Positioning and related work

## Scope

This repository does not propose a new general ontology of provenance, a new theory of scientific authorship, or a representative benchmark of AI reliability.

Its object is narrower: **longitudinal claim-state transitions in AI-assisted mathematical research**.

The unit of observation is not the conversation as a whole but a sourced transition affecting a mathematical or scientific claim.

## What is already established

The project is rooted in several existing traditions:

- generic provenance models such as W3C PROV-O;
- scientific claim and evidence representations, including micropublications and nanopublications;
- reproducibility and FAIR-style research-object practices;
- contribution and accountability taxonomies;
- audit trails and provenance for agentic / AI-assisted workflows;
- claim-change analysis across versions of scientific documents;
- human–AI collaboration datasets and longitudinal interaction studies.

These traditions mean that neither “provenance”, “claim-level representation”, nor “audit trail” is claimed as new here.

## Residual contribution studied here

The empirical delta is the explicit longitudinal coding of **epistemic transitions of mathematical claims**, including negative and corrective events that are often lost in final publications:

- proposal;
- human veto;
- failed or non-promoting statistical test;
- restriction of scope;
- exact finite verification;
- refutation;
- documentary regression;
- provenance restoration;
- release;
- later reclassification as classical or non-novel.

The protocol keeps separate:

1. epistemic status;
2. documentary status;
3. diffusion status;
4. workflow status;
5. source-relative evidence status.

A publication event is therefore not automatically an epistemic transition, and a documentary correction is not automatically a mathematical correction.

## Why this corpus may still be informative

The corpus is small and non-representative. Its potential value lies in temporal and documentary resolution rather than scale.

It records intermediate decisions and demotions, not only successful outputs. This makes it suitable for studying how claims are promoted, blocked, corrected, re-scoped, or re-attributed during AI-assisted mathematical work.

## Novelty boundary

The project claims no priority for provenance, claim graphs, audit trails, scientific accountability frameworks, or longitudinal human–AI studies.

Any originality claim is limited to the specific protocol/corpus combination and must remain conditional on continued prior-art review.

A negative literature search is not evidence of novelty.
